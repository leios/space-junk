# A story I know you knew

Hey Dad,

On the topic of divulging secrets, here is a story that I know you know, but you don't know that I know you know.
You know?

Well, until you read this.
Then you'll know that I knew you knew the whole time.

Right.
I think that makes sense.
This is the story of the first time I left Nemo.
No, not on the boat.
With Jake.

One afternoon, I stopped by the pool deck for a quick workout.
I wasn't planning on doing anything crazy, maybe a few thousand meters or something.
After getting ready, I peeked out of the changing room to see if anyone else was in the water and I saw you and Jake.

You were sprawled on the ground and immobile while Jake was leaning over and asking if you were alright.
He reached toward you and said, "C'mon, one more set and we are done."

You slapped the hand away and said, "Get out of here man. I'm out."

The two of you then laughed a bit before Jake said, "Aight. I'mma go a bit longer." 
He then checked the holographic clock before quickly exhaling and shouting, "On the top!"
Then there was a splash and he was gone.
To be honest, he was so quick that I didn't even see him move.

About a minute later, you launched yourself up and began looking around before your eyes settled on me.
You smiled and chuckled, looking back at Jake, "Would you believe there was a time I was faster than him?"

I walked over without really saying anything, so you continued a bit.

"He had a dream and stuck to it.
Your mom did too."
You then laid back on the ground and groaned a bit.
"Me on the other hand... I just want to sleep."

Around this time, Jake was finishing a lap and called over to me. "Hey Rei! Thanks for the goggles! They are much clearer than the old ones I made."

I called back, "Yeah, no problem! You and Grampa Jeb did like 90% of the work. I just finished up the old blueprints I found in lab."

Jake nodded a bit, clearly distracted by the clock.
Before pushing off, he said, "Well, I'll keep going for a bit. Let's talk later."

I sat next to you for a moment while formulating my workout.
You then said, "Dad was also faster than him at some point. Heck, your mom was faster when he arrived on Nemo. We taught him how to swim in the first place!"
You stretched and yawned without getting up. "Now he's here lapping us. Lapping everyone really."

I tucked my hair in my cap and put on my goggles, only to see maybe 7 or 8 screens hovering around you.
At this point, I knew better than to ask about what I saw, but one image caught my eye.
It looked like a belt with a few propellers attached to it.

I couldn't quite piece together what it was for, so I tried to look at it a bit longer while continuing the conversation.
"Well, you taught me to swim too. Maybe I'll be lapping Jake soon enough."

You laughed, "No way that's possible."
But you paused, looking at the belt again before continuing.
"Your Grampa tried to keep up and ended up nearly dying. It's not worth it. Let Jake be Jake. You be you."

I didn't really know what to say and figured there wasn't much more I could learn from the screens at this point, so we sat there in awkward silence for a bit before I said, "Well, I'm going to swim now."

You: "Right. I'm going to shower. See you at home."

Me: "See you soon."

I then sat next to the blocks and dangled my feet into the water, just listening to Jake splashing around while creating ripples with my toes in my lane.
When he got to the wall he looked towards me for a second before checking to see if you had left.
He then sighed and said, "How about a technique day?"
So we worked on butterfly and sidestroke a bit before getting out.

Apparently, he had already been swimming for 2 hours by the time I got there and was looking for some sort of cooldown.
But a cooldown for him was rather intense for me.
I am sure he is still way faster than anyone else on the island.
I know everyone is older now, but I can't imagine Jake has stopped swimming.
In fact, I bet that right now the two of you are at the pool goofing around.

After my shower, I met Jake outside of the locker rooms.
He began asking me very specific questions about how I got the goggles to work.
I don't remember all the questions he asked, but I do distinctly remember that some of them were already solved by the time I found the blueprints.
I'm not entirely sure who modified the design before me, but I would guess it was either you or Grampa Jeb.

At some point, we ended up at the dock, just talking about life.
I asked a bit about his research.
He asked a bit about mine.
Then he started talking about you.
He said, "Your dad is special. He does things. No matter what you throw at him, he'll do it. And he always does a good job. Do you know how hard it is to do a good job at *everything*. He's never the best, but second place. Second place is incredible when you think about it."

There wasn't much to say other than, "Yeah, I guess that's why he basically runs this place."

"Not *basically*. He *does*."
Jake then looked off towards the horizon.
"He and Jeb were an amazing duo.
Not too different than you and him.
I'm glad the family has stuck together."

I've always believed that facts are somewhat subjective.
Two people can experience the same thing and feel completely different about the events.
Jake saw a father teaching his son everything.
He then saw that son passing it on to his daughter.
I saw it slightly differently.
So I said something.
A truth.
But one I regret.

"What family? I've never met Grampa Jeb. I've almost never seen my mom except through holo calls. And dad..."
I sighed before completing the sentence in my head *is always too busy being second best.*

Jake was silent for a moment before also sighing and leaning back onto his hands.
Jake then said...

> When your dad and I were younger, we had a little Nemo swim team.
> This was well before we built the pool, so we would just swim around different parts of the island.
> Under bridges and whatnot.
> At the time, your dad was way, way faster than me.
> Jeb was faster too.
> I was lapped, and then lapped again.
> And again.

> No matter how hard I failed, the two of them kept motivating me to push on.
> So I did.
> I stopped walking around and started swimming instead.
> I tried to incoporate working out into my daily life.
> Something simple at the top of every hour.
> Stretching before bed.
> I did everything I could.

> And it still wasn't enough.

> After practice one day, I was chilling with Jeb and straight-up asked him, "Hey. Do I have any shot of actually catching up?"

> He reponded immediately, "Jake. You're going to be faster than both of us. I guarantee it."

> I then asked him, "How though? I'm short. I don't have the same equipment as the two of you."

> Your grampa was a good man.
> He thought for a second, and then said, "Look around Jake. We are on a floating island in the middle of no where. No one has the right equipment. We make it work."

> I don't know why I'm saying this.
> That was the moment everything changed for me.
> I started seeing everyone on the island differently.

> At that point in my life, I had been disowned by everyone in my family except my parents.
> And at times, I felt unwelcome even at home.
> Yet your dad, your mom, Jeb...
> They accepted me openly.
> Never questioned me.
> Always encouraged me.

> I think that's what I mean by "family"
> It's a vibe.
> That's what I'm glad is being passed along.
> I don't know how to describe it.
> Openness?
> Selflessness?
> It doesn't matter.

> You know what I'm talking about, right?
> I'm not crazy?

To be honest, I did kinda think he was crazy, but I didn't say that at the time.
I think I just queitly agreed.
Probably just sat there awkwardly for a while.

Jake then kept going...

> A few years later, we had a pool built and I was fast.
> Real fast.
> Faster than anyone else on the island.
> Faster than most people in the world.

> Jeb's training was harsh.
> But he made it a point to always swim next to me.
> He used to say, "You might be swimming in your own lane, but you are never alone."
> Our practices wouldn't end unless I lapped him at least once.

> The problem was that he cheated.
> He would use fins.

> He made me wear drag suits.
> Anything to push me further.

> It's a bit embarrassing, but even now, I'll project a person slightly faster than I am in the lane next to me while swimming.
> It's a great way to motivate you to go even faster.

> Eventually I caught up to him even after he exhausted all of his tricks..
> So he invented things.
> It started with a kickboard with a propeller built into it and ended with something he called a "superfin."
> I don't know what it was exactly.
> Something like a belt with propellers attached to the side.
> He never finished it because...

Jake then sighed and stared off into space.
"He just never finished it."

I got the gist.
Grampa was lost before he the design was finalized.
I then thought out oud, "Hey. What if I finish the superfin for him? For you? A race sounds like a lot of fun actually."

I don't know why I suggested it, exactly.
Maybe because I wanted to figure out what you were looking at earlier.
Maybe because I wanted to give Jake a sense of closure.

No matter the case, Jake laughed and said, "Yeah. That would be fun. I'm happy to help in any way I can!"

So I got to work building the superfin, but when I got to lab, I noticed there were no blueprints available.
I guessed that you hid anything you saw as dangerous after the drone incident.
I could have just stolen them from you.
After all, I had all your files.
But instead I decided build it from scratch while loosely following what I saw at the pool.

As it turns out, it's not easy to just "attach boat propellers to a person while still allowing them to swim naturally."
While swimming, you can rotate lengthwise, like in front and back crawl.
Or you can rotate around your stomach like in butterfly and breaststroke.
Or both.
It's hard to allow the swimmer to move in any direction they want while also staying at the surface of the water.

I tried a bunch of different designs.
Most of them failed.
The ones that worked weren't nearly fast enough to keep up with Jake.
But we finally got everything right by mock 12.

Well, it was "working," but not really.
I mean, the pool's only 50 meters.
No matter how hard I tried, I couldn't figure out how to change momentum quickly for the turn.
The superfin mock 12 could go really fast, but only in one direction.

At first, Jake said, "No problem. Let's just race around the island."

I wasn't super keen on that idea in the case that you found out about our secret project, so Jake instead said, "What about a race to the mangroves?"

At that point in my life, I hadn't heard about the mangroves.
All I knew was that there was a large barrier separating the water around Nemo from the rest of the ocean.
This was apparently necessary or else Nemo would be fighting incredibly large ocean swells.

Jake said the idea was largely inspired by mangrove regions on the mainland, where an interconnected network of tree roots dampen the waves before they hit the shore.
Obviously, there are no trees on Nemo, so we transformed a large portion of the Pacific plastic into an itnerconnected web of fibers to dampen the waves coming in.
Typhoons would still get through, of course, but this made Nemo mostly impervious to weather.

Now, at the time, I should have recognized how dangerous it was to do this.
Well, Jake should have recognized it anyway.
He was an adult.
You, Jake, mom, and Grampa Jeb had all been out there many times.
I can't even begin to imagine why he thought this was a good idea.

But without knowing any better, I agreed.

Now, it wasn't like we went out without any safety precautions...
